## Session Overview

The learner retold The Magic Finger by Roald Dahl, about a girl whose magic finger turns her hunting neighbors into small winged creatures. They discussed the story's message about how we treat animals, found its mix of humor and seriousness surprising, and said they want to keep reading to see how the family changes back.

## 💬 Idioms

### “at the same time”

simultaneously; both things true or happening together

- *She felt nervous and excited at the same time.*
- *Two people can't speak at the same time, so let's take turns.*

### “back to normal”

returned to the usual or regular condition

- *After the power came back on, the town slowly got back to normal.*
- *I hope my sleep schedule goes back to normal next week.*

### “instead of”

in place of; rather than

- *Let's walk instead of taking the bus.*
- *She ordered tea instead of coffee.*

## 📚 Vocabulary to Remember

- **tingle** — to feel a slight prickling or buzzing sensation on the skin
  - *My hand began to tingle after I held the cold ice.*
- **spell** — magic words or actions that are believed to make something magical happen
  - *The witch cast a spell that turned the prince into a frog.*
- **hide** — to put yourself or something where others cannot see or find it
  - *The cat likes to hide under the bed when guests arrive.*
- **serious** — important or significant; not joking or light
  - *The teacher's face looked serious when she announced the test.*
- **treat** — to behave toward someone or something in a particular way
  - *Dogs should always be treated gently.*
- **next door** — in the house or building right beside your own
  - *The family next door always waves when I walk by.*

## 🔧 Say It Better

1. ❌ *she can put a magic on people*
   ✅ **she can put a spell on people**
   💡 Magic is uncountable here; use 'a spell' with 'put' or 'cast'.
2. ❌ *she put a spell to the Gregg family*
   ✅ **she put a spell on the Gregg family**
   💡 Use 'on' (not 'to') when a spell is placed on someone.
3. ❌ *Next morning, the family woke up*
   ✅ **The next morning, the family woke up**
   💡 Time expressions like 'the next morning' usually need 'the'.
4. ❌ *I was surprising how funny and also serious the story is*
   ✅ **I was surprised by how funny and also serious the story is**
   💡 Use -ed adjectives (surprised, excited) to describe how a person feels; -ing adjectives describe the thing causing the feeling.
5. ❌ *how the family change back to normal*
   ✅ **how the family changes back to normal**
   💡 Singular subjects like 'the family' take a third-person -s verb in the present tense.
6. ❌ *I want to read more tomorrow to know how*
   ✅ **I want to read more tomorrow to find out how**
   💡 'Find out' is the natural phrase for learning information you didn't know.

## ✅ Check Yourself

**Q1.** After the power came back on, the town slowly got ____.

- back to normal
- instead of
- hide

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>back to normal</strong> — 'Back to normal' means returned to the usual state; 'instead of' and 'hide' do not fit this sentence.</p></details>

**Q2.** We should eat fruit ____ cake tonight because it is healthier.

- instead of
- next door
- spell

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>instead of</strong> — 'Instead of' introduces the choice you make in place of another; 'next door' describes location and 'spell' is a magic word.</p></details>

**Q3.** My best friend lives ____, so I can visit her whenever I want.

- next door
- serious
- treat

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>next door</strong> — 'Next door' describes a home right beside yours; 'serious' is an adjective and 'treat' is a verb.</p></details>

**Q4.** The weather forecast said the storm was ____, so we should stay home.

- serious
- spell
- tingle

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>serious</strong> — 'Serious' describes something important or dangerous; 'spell' and 'tingle' do not describe a storm's strength.</p></details>

**Q5.** The children tried to ____ behind the sofa when they heard the doorbell.

- hide
- treat
- at the same time

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>hide</strong> — 'Hide' means to conceal yourself from view; 'treat' describes how you behave toward others, and 'at the same time' is a phrase about timing.</p></details>

**Q6.** Please ____ your friends with kindness, because everyone deserves respect.

- treat
- tingle
- back to normal

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>treat</strong> — 'Treat' means to behave toward people in a certain way; 'tingle' is a physical sensation and 'back to normal' describes a return to usual conditions.</p></details>
