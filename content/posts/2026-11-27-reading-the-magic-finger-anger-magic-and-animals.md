## Session Overview

A learner summarized the first eighteen pages of a children's story about a girl whose anger gives her a magic finger. They talked about how the story treats animals and hunting, and how it mixes humor with serious themes. They also shared a memory of seeing hunters as a child and said they want to keep reading.

## 💬 Idioms

### “put a spell on”

use magic on someone to change them or control them

- *The witch put a spell on the prince and turned him into a frog.*
- *Stop looking at me like that, or I'll put a spell on you!*

### “at the same time”

happening together, or both true together

- *She is funny and honest at the same time.*
- *We can cook dinner and watch the news at the same time.*

### “change back to normal”

return to how things were before

- *After the holiday, my sleep schedule slowly changed back to normal.*
- *The printer should change back to normal after you restart it.*

## 📚 Vocabulary to Remember

- **tingle** — a light, prickling feeling on the skin
  - *My hand began to tingle after I held the cold metal for too long.*
- **hide** — go or put yourself somewhere so people cannot see you
  - *The kids tried to hide behind the sofa during the game.*
- **serious** — not joking; thoughtful, or important in a way that needs attention
  - *The coach's voice was serious when he explained the new rules.*
- **childhood** — the time when a person is a child
  - *Her childhood was full of long summers by the sea.*
- **surprised** — feeling astonished because something unexpected happened
  - *I was surprised to find so many old photos in the attic.*
- **hunt** — chase and kill wild animals for food or sport
  - *Some people hunt wild animals, but many countries have strict rules about it.*

## 🔧 Say It Better

1. ❌ *she can put a magic on people*
   ✅ **she can put a spell on people**
   💡 We say 'put a spell on' someone, not 'put a magic on'.
2. ❌ *they like hunting ducks every Saturday*
   ✅ **they like to hunt ducks every Saturday**
   💡 'Like' is usually followed by 'to + verb' or 'verb-ing' for a general habit; 'to hunt' is more natural here.
3. ❌ *she put a spell to the Gregg family*
   ✅ **she put a spell on the Gregg family**
   💡 The preposition after 'spell' is 'on', not 'to'.
4. ❌ *Next morning, the family woke up*
   ✅ **The next morning, the family woke up**
   💡 Time expressions like 'the next morning' usually need 'the'.
5. ❌ *I was surprising how funny and also serious the story is*
   ✅ **I was surprised by how funny and also serious the story is**
   💡 Use '-ed' adjectives (surprised, amazed) to describe how you feel; '-ing' adjectives (surprising) describe the thing.
6. ❌ *how the family change back to normal*
   ✅ **how the family changes back to normal**
   💡 After 'how the family' (a singular noun), the verb needs '-s' in the present tense.

## ✅ Check Yourself

**Q1.** The grandmother joked that she could ____ the whole family if they ate all the cookies.

- put a spell on
- serious
- childhood

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>put a spell on</strong> — 'Put a spell on' means use magic on someone; 'serious' and 'childhood' are not verbs that take a direct object like this.</p></details>

**Q2.** The coach was ____ about the rules, but he also smiled a lot.

- serious
- childhood
- surprised

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>serious</strong> — 'Serious' describes a manner that is not joking; the other two words don't fit the description of his tone.</p></details>

**Q3.** When the rain started, we ran to ____ under the old bridge.

- hide
- hunt
- tingle

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>hide</strong> — 'Run to hide' means run to get out of sight; 'hunt' and 'tingle' do not describe going somewhere to be unseen.</p></details>

**Q4.** After the long trip, it took a week for our sleep schedule to ____.

- change back to normal
- at the same time
- put a spell on

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>change back to normal</strong> — 'To change back to normal' means return to the usual state; the other two phrases don't describe recovery.</p></details>

**Q5.** My favorite photos are from my ____, when we spent every summer at the lake.

- childhood
- hunt
- surprised

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>childhood</strong> — 'Childhood' is a noun for the years when someone was a child; 'hunt' and 'surprised' don't work as nouns for a time period.</p></details>

**Q6.** She was completely ____ when she saw the new house.

- surprised
- serious
- tingle

<details><summary>Show answer</summary><p><strong>surprised</strong> — 'Surprised' is an adjective for a reaction to something unexpected; 'serious' and 'tingle' don't describe that reaction.</p></details>
