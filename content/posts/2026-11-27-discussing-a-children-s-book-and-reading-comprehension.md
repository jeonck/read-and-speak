## Session Overview

Two learners work through a passage about a family of birds who must build a nest after a group of dogs takes over their house, then talk about how reading comprehensible input can support speaking. They also test a reading-practice website and discuss its transcription quality. The session mixes story retelling, comprehension questions, and a short look at the app's features.

## 💬 Idioms

### “come up with”

to think of or produce an idea, plan, or solution

- *They had to come up with a plan to get back into the house.*
- *Can you come up with a better title for this book?*

### “break the ice”

to start a conversation or make people feel comfortable with each other

- *Reading a story aloud is a good way to break the ice in a new study group.*
- *A funny question can break the ice at the start of a conversation partner session.*

### “make sense of something”

to understand something that is confusing or unclear

- *I couldn't make sense of the last chapter until I reread the first one.*
- *The transcript was messy, but I could still make sense of the main story.*

## 📚 Vocabulary to Remember

- **nest** — a structure built by birds from sticks, leaves, and feathers to lay eggs and rest in
  - *The birds spent three days building a nest at the top of the tree.*
- **sob** — to cry with short, loud, breathing sounds
  - *She began to sob when she realized the house was gone.*
- **occupy** — to take over a place and be using it
  - *The dogs occupied the whole house while the family was outside.*
- **comprehensible** — easy enough to understand
  - *Listening to slow, comprehensible speech helps beginners improve.*
- **hover** — to stay in one place in the air, moving only slightly
  - *The bird hovered above the nest before landing.*
- **beastly** — very unpleasant or cruel, often used in an old-fashioned way
  - *What a beastly thing to say to a friend!*
- **mince** — to chop something into very small pieces
  - *She minced the garlic before adding it to the sauce.*

## 🔧 Say It Better

1. ❌ *Mr. Gregg took the sticks and started to build the nest.*
   ✅ **Mr. Gregg took the sticks and started to build the nest.**
   💡 This sentence is already correct; it is a good model of past simple narration.
2. ❌ *I wanna sleep in my house bed.*
   ✅ **I want to sleep in my bed at home.**
   💡 Use 'want to' instead of 'wanna' in writing, and 'at home' instead of 'house' for a place.
3. ❌ *They have taken our house.*
   ✅ **They have taken over our house.**
   💡 'Take over' means to gain control of a place or thing.
4. ❌ *We have no place to go.*
   ✅ **We have nowhere to go.**
   💡 'Nowhere' is the natural word for 'no place'.
5. ❌ *Mrs. Gregg took the two boys under her wings and hugged them.*
   ✅ **Mrs. Gregg took the two boys under her wings and hugged them.**
   💡 This is correct; 'under her wings' is a figurative phrase for protecting someone.
6. ❌ *The 1st one put out a hand and opened the door.*
   ✅ **The first one put out a hand and opened the door.**
   💡 Spell out ordinal numbers in narration, like 'first' instead of '1st'.
7. ❌ *I can show some sentences and For example.*
   ✅ **I can show you some sentences, for example.**
   💡 Add 'you' after 'show' when naming the person receiving the action.
8. ❌ *I'm transcribed, my readings.*
   ✅ **My reading is being transcribed.**
   💡 Use the passive 'is being transcribed' for an action happening to something.
9. ❌ *Yesterday, the family was changed into bird.*
   ✅ **Yesterday, the family was changed into birds.**
   💡 Use the plural 'birds' when talking about more than one creature.
