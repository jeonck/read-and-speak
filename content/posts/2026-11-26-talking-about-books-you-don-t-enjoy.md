## Today's Idiom

> **not my cup of tea**

There was no full session transcript today, so this lesson centers on the idiom "not my cup of tea" and related expressions for talking about likes, dislikes, and gradual changes in taste. Learners practice describing books, plots, and reactions in a natural, conversational way.

## 💬 Idioms

### “not my cup of tea”

Something you don't like or aren't interested in. It's a polite way to say a book, film, or activity doesn't appeal to you.

- *Horror films are not my cup of tea, so I usually pick comedies.*
- *Opera is not my cup of tea, but I respect people who love it.*

### “to grow on someone”

To become gradually more liked or appreciated over time, after you didn't like it at first.

- *The book was slow at first, but the characters grew on me by the last chapter.*
- *That album didn't impress me at first, but it's really grown on me.*

### “to be into something”

To be interested in or enthusiastic about something.

- *She's really into mystery novels these days.*
- *I'm not really into poetry, but I enjoyed this collection.*

## 📚 Vocabulary to Remember

- **gripping** — So exciting or interesting that you can't stop paying attention.
  - *The first chapter was so gripping that I read until midnight.*
- **predictable** — Easy to guess in advance, so it's not surprising.
  - *The villain's identity was predictable, which made the ending less exciting.*
- **lukewarm** — Not very enthusiastic or excited; showing little interest.
  - *The critics gave the novel a lukewarm review.*
- **page-turner** — A book that is so exciting you want to keep reading it quickly.
  - *This thriller is a real page-turner; I finished it in one weekend.*
- **plot** — The main sequence of events in a story.
  - *The plot of the novel follows two sisters who uncover a family secret.*

## 🔧 Say It Better

1. ❌ *not my cup of tea*
   ✅ **It's not my cup of tea.**
   💡 In writing or formal speech, add a subject such as "It's" or "That's" to make a complete sentence; the idiom itself often stands alone in casual talk.
